# References

Full source list behind Silicon-Sample's README, with more detail on what each source did and why it matters here. Organized: foundational papers, recent calibration methods, Google / Google DeepMind work, bibliography, and commercial landscape.

## Foundational papers

**Argyle, L.P., Busby, E.C., Fulda, N., Gubler, J.R., Rytting, C., & Wingate, D. (2023). ["Out of One, Many: Using Language Models to Simulate Human Samples."](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/out-of-one-many-using-language-models-to-simulate-human-samples/035D7C8A55B237942FB6DBAD7CAA4E49) *Political Analysis*, 31(3), 337–351. (Preprint: [arXiv:2209.06899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06899).)**
The origin of the term "silicon sampling." Conditions GPT-3 on thousands of ANES (2012/2016/2020) sociodemographic backstories and shows the resulting synthetic samples reproduce not just marginal response rates but cross-item correlations and subgroup nuance - coining **algorithmic fidelity** as the measure of how much a model's internal representation of a subpopulation, versus surface mimicry, drives its outputs. This is the direct conceptual ancestor of Silicon-Sample's whole approach: condition on real respondent rows, then check whether the model actually captures subgroup structure or just gives plausible-sounding answers.

**Durmus, E., Nguyen, K., Liao, T.I., Schiefer, N., Askell, A., Bakhtin, A., Chen, C., Hatfield-Dodds, Z., Hernandez, D., Joseph, N., Lovitt, L., McCandlish, S., Sikder, O., Tamkin, A., Thamkul, J., Kaplan, J., Clark, J., & Ganguli, D. (2024, Anthropic). ["Towards Measuring the Representation of Subjective Global Opinions in Language Models."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16388)**
Builds **GlobalOpinionQA**, a benchmark comparing LLM outputs to the World Values Survey and Pew Research's Global Attitudes Survey. Tests three prompting setups (default, country-specific, and language-translated) on an RLHF + Constitutional-AI-tuned model. Key finding: by default, responses resemble US/European/South American opinions most; prompting the model to adopt a country's perspective shifts responses toward that country but can amplify cultural stereotypes; and translating the question into a country's language does *not* reliably make responses match that country's real opinions. Directly relevant to Silicon-Sample's WVS component - flags non-US subgroups as higher-risk for calibration than GSS/ANES.

**Park, J.S., Zou, C.Q., Shaw, A., Hill, B.M., Cai, C., Morris, M.R., Willer, R., Liang, P., & Bernstein, M.S. (2024, Stanford). ["Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109) (See also the [Stanford HAI policy brief](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/simulating-human-behavior-with-ai-agents).)**
Conditions each of 1,052 real-person agents on a full two-hour qualitative interview transcript, rather than a demographic tag, then replays social-science survey batteries (including GSS items) and experimental replications. Agents reproduce their real counterpart's later answers ~85% as accurately as the person reproduces their own answers two weeks apart - the strongest fidelity number in this literature - and the architecture reduces accuracy bias across racial and ideological groups compared to demographic-only prompting. Same target population size (~1,000) as this project; a natural stretch-goal ablation is testing richer respondent narratives (using GSS/ANES/WVS free-text fields) against thin demographic prompts, using this paper's result as the reason to expect a fidelity gain.

**Bisbee, J., Clinton, J., Dorff, C., Kenkel, B., & Larson, J. (2024). ["Synthetic Replacements for Human Survey Data? The Perils of Large Language Models."](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/synthetic-replacements-for-human-survey-data-the-perils-of-large-language-models/B92267DC26195C7F36E63EA04A47D2FE) *Political Analysis*, 32(4), 401–416.**
Evaluates persona-prompted ChatGPT 3.5 Turbo on the standard ANES feeling-thermometer item (partisan/racial affect). Finds synthetic partisan and racial affective-polarization estimates ~7x larger than the real average for matching human respondents, and - the critical number - synthetic standard deviation only ~31% of the variance found among real humans with the same attributes. This is the single most important cautionary result for Silicon-Sample: it is the specific failure mode (variance collapse) the project's MAE + distributional-distance scoring step needs to catch, not just mean-matching.

**Kim, J., & Lee, B. (2024). ["AI-Augmented Surveys: Leveraging Large Language Models and Surveys for Opinion Prediction."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09620)**
Uses General Social Survey data (1972–2021, binarized questions) for two tasks: *retrodiction* (predicting a missing year/question cell for a respondent who exists in the data) and *unasked-opinion prediction* (predicting an entirely unasked question). Fine-tuned Alpaca-7B gets AUC 0.73, ρ 0.67 on the harder unasked-opinion task, and the paper is able to pinpoint historical inflection points in opinion (e.g., rising support for same-sex marriage) from model predictions alone. This is the closest existing pipeline to Silicon-Sample's own two-phase design (calibrate on known history, then predict unasked questions) and the best available benchmark numbers for the "run new questions" phase.

## Other papers considered

**Santurkar, S., Durmus, E., Ladhak, F., Lee, C., Liang, P., & Hashimoto, T. (2023). ["Whose Opinions Do Language Models Reflect?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17548)**
Builds the OpinionQA benchmark on Pew's American Trends Panels, scoring base vs. RLHF-tuned models against 60 US demographic groups at the distribution level. Confirms left-leaning skew in some human-feedback-tuned models and surfaces groups poorly represented across the board (e.g., 65+, widowed individuals).

**Dominguez-Olmedo, R., Hardt, M., & Mendler-Dünner, C. (2023). ["Questioning the Survey Responses of Large Language Models."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07951)**
Tests 39 models of various sizes on 25 multiple-choice items from the 2019 American Community Survey. Finds that, robust to prompt variation, aggregate response statistics trend toward *uniform-random* rather than a real opinion distribution - i.e., a naive model may show no real signal at all. Useful as a null-hypothesis / sanity check for Silicon-Sample's scoring step: beating uniform-random is a necessary, not sufficient, bar.

**Sun, S., Lee, E., Nan, D., Zhao, X., Lee, W., Jansen, B.J., & Kim, J.H. (2024). ["Random Silicon Sampling: Simulating Human Sub-Population Opinion Using a Large Language Model Based on Group-Level Demographic Information."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18144)**
Extends Argyle et al.'s method by sampling agents from group-level demographic *distributions* rather than bootstrapping individual real respondent rows. Close cousin to the "population sampler" step in Silicon-Sample's pipeline; worth comparing against row-bootstrapping to see which sampling strategy calibrates better per subgroup.

**Sanders, N.E., Ulinich, A., & Schneier, B. (2023). ["Demonstrations of the Potential of AI-based Political Issue Polling."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04781)**
GPT-3.5-turbo tested against Cooperative Election Study questions on policy issues (abortion, Supreme Court approval, etc.). Strong correlation (typically >85%) on ideological breakdown, weaker on demographic-level differences, and a specific documented failure: the model overgeneralizes to policy issues that arose after its training cutoff (e.g., support for US involvement in Ukraine). Directly cautionary for Silicon-Sample's Phase 04 - new questions about genuinely novel topics are higher-risk than new questions on already-familiar themes.

## Recent calibration methods (2025–2026)

- **["Assessing the Reliability of Persona-Conditioned LLMs as Synthetic Survey Respondents"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18462)** - reliability and variance analysis of the exact persona-prompt approach this project uses; a direct sanity check before committing to a prompt-only design.
- **["Specializing Large Language Models to Simulate Survey Response Distributions for Global Populations"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07068)** - fine-tuning approach that materially cuts distributional error on WVS-style, multi-country data; relevant if prompting alone underperforms on WVS.
- **["Distribution Shift Alignment Helps LLMs Simulate Survey Response Distributions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21977)** - an alignment method aimed specifically at closing the gap between a model's default response distribution and a target real distribution.
- **["Valid Survey Simulations with Limited Human Data: The Roles of Prompting, Fine-Tuning, and Rectification"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11408)** - compares prompting vs. fine-tuning vs. post-hoc statistical rectification; reports fine-tuning-on-domain-data getting error under 3% in some settings, and offers a rectification recipe that could substitute for a full fine-tune under a hackathon timebox.
- **["Mitigating Social Desirability Bias in Random Silicon Sampling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22725)** - targets the specific, repeatedly-documented failure mode where models shift toward the "acceptable" answer on sensitive topics (race, gender, identity politics) rather than the real, more polarized human distribution.
- **Kalinin, K. (2023). ["Improving GPT Generated Synthetic Samples with Sampling-Permutation Algorithm."](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4548937)** Uses the Survey of Russian Elites (1993–2020) with GPT-3 and proposes a prompting/aggregation method for pulling generated samples closer to real survey means - a concrete technique usable in the template-sweep step of the calibration loop.

Broader curated bibliography (US-centric, German, Russian, and cross-country papers): [CaroHaensch/public_opinion_llms](https://github.com/CaroHaensch/public_opinion_llms).

## Google / Google DeepMind

- **Google DeepMind - [Concordia](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia)** ([Vezhnevets et al., "Generative agent-based modeling with actions grounded in physical, social, or digital space using Concordia," arXiv:2312.03664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)). An open-source library for generative agent-based social simulation: agents take grounded actions inside an environment run by a "Game Master" entity, which translates natural-language intentions into outcomes. Not silicon-sampling specific (built more for multi-agent interaction than static survey-answering), but a candidate framework for structuring Silicon-Sample's agent population and evaluation harness if the project wants more than single-turn Q&A.
- **Google DeepMind - Tessler, M., et al. (2024). ["AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation."](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adq2852)** *Science*. The "Habermas Machine": an AI system that mediates group discussions by iteratively generating consensus statements from individual opinions and critiques, tested on 5,700 UK participants; the AI-generated statements were preferred by participants slightly more often than human-mediator statements. Different goal from Silicon-Sample (building consensus vs. matching a real distribution), but adjacent territory - modeling and aggregating populations of opinions with AI.
- **Google (UX Research team). ["Artificial Data, Real Insights: Evaluating Opportunities and Risks of Expanding the Data Ecosystem with Synthetic Data."](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15260)** Includes a case study benchmarking GPT-4 and Gemini Pro 1.5 personas (matched on age, gender, product usage) against 500 real human respondents from Qualtrics panels on 8 technology trust/privacy attitude questions. The closest of the three Google-affiliated sources to Silicon-Sample's actual calibration setup, though it's a Google UX research paper rather than a DeepMind-branded one, and no DeepMind paper was found doing the specific "calibrate on historical survey, then predict unasked questions" loop that defines this project.

## Commercial landscape

**[Aaru](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/ai-synthetic-research-startup-aaru-raised-a-series-a-at-a-1b-headline-valuation/)** - synthetic-audience startup founded March 2024 (Cameron Fink, Ned Koh, John Kessler). Generates thousands of AI agents from demographic, psychographic, and proprietary data to simulate consumer/voter behavior. Closed a multi-tier Series A led by Redpoint Ventures at a ~$1B "headline" valuation (Dec 2025); accurately predicted the NYC Democratic primary within 371 votes; clients include McDonald's, Boston Beer, A24, Bayer, and Spindrift (replicated a 500-person, two-month traditional survey result in one week for a product-line decision). The strongest evidence this category has real commercial pull - but Aaru and comparable products (Lakmoos AI, InstaPoll/Arima) do not publish calibration methodology against public survey ground truth (GSS/ANES/WVS) the way the academic papers above do, which is the gap Silicon-Sample's approach is positioned in.
